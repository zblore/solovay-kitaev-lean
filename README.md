# The Solovay–Kitaev theorem, in Lean

A Lean 4 proof of the Solovay–Kitaev theorem for single-qubit Clifford+T circuits, with Dawson and Nielsen's exponent:

A word is a finite sequence of the gates H, T and T⁻¹, and its value is the product of their 2 × 2 matrices. There are constants K > 0 and e > 0 such that, for every ε with 0 < ε ≤ e, every U in SU(2) is within ε of the value of a word of length at most K · log(1/ε)^c, where c = log 5 / log(3/2) ≈ 3.97.

Distances are in the operator norm. The word approximates U itself, not U up to a phase. Any unitary is a phase times an element of SU(2), so this covers every single-qubit gate up to phase.

The repository proves two statements:

- `solovay_kitaev`: the theorem above.
- `exponent_mem_Ioo`: the exponent c lies strictly between 3 and 4.

The constants K and e aren't computed. They come from compactness of SU(2) and continuity of the determinant. The exponent is exact. Counting T⁻¹ as one letter only affects K: writing each T⁻¹ in a word as T⁷ gives a word over H and T alone, at most seven times as long.

Solovay announced the theorem for SU(2) in 1995 and didn't publish a proof. Kitaev outlined a proof in a 1997 review paper. Dawson and Nielsen wrote up the algorithm, with the exponent used here, in 2006. The theorem is classical and I claim no priority.

## Other Lean proofs

Palomar has no other entry for this theorem. I know of one other Lean proof:

- [`spitters/CatCrypt-core`](https://github.com/spitters/CatCrypt-core/tree/5720e6afceabeea66e5764095b829b7fdb0ea6da/CatCryptCore/ForMathlib/SolovayKitaev), by Bas Spitters and the CatCrypt contributors, committed on 16 November 2026. Its `solovay_kitaev` proves the theorem for SU(2) over any gate set closed under inverses, assuming a net of words of bounded length, with explicit constants and the same exponent. Its `HTDense.lean` proves that H and T, scaled to determinant one, generate a dense subgroup of SU(2). The two aren't combined into a theorem about Clifford+T words there.

I haven't compiled it. This proof is independent of it. It comes from my csd-lean4 library, where I proved it on 6 October 2026 ([commit `01e973e9`](https://github.com/zblore/csd-lean4/commit/01e973e95ee84ef98d7697ab5921f2286532684f)).

## The proof

The proof follows Dawson and Nielsen's recursion, as cited in csd-lean4. I haven't read their paper.

1. **Density.** The closure of the group generated by H and T contains SU(2). The rotation angle of T·HTH is an irrational multiple of 2π, so its powers fill a circle of rotations, up to phase. A second word gives a second circle, with its own irrationality proof. Conjugating by Z = T⁴ gives a third axis at right angles to the second, and an Euler decomposition about that pair covers SU(2).
2. **A finite net.** SU(2) is compact, so for any ε₀ > 0 finitely many words come within ε₀ of every element.
3. **One level of the recursion.** Approximate U by a word u. The residual U·u⁻¹ is close to the identity, and it has determinant one, because the determinant of a word is an eighth root of unity and the only one near 1 is 1. Write the residual as a group commutator V·W·V⁻¹·W⁻¹ of two unitaries close to the identity, approximate V and W one level down, and reassemble. The error goes from ε to about ε^(3/2).
4. **The count.** One level uses five shorter words, and inverting a word doesn't lengthen it. So level n has length at most 5ⁿ times the net's length, and error about ε₀^((3/2)ⁿ). Eliminating n gives the length bound K · log(1/ε)^(log 5 / log(3/2)).

## Files

- `Challenge.lean` states the two theorems and their definitions: the T gate's phase `tPhase`, the gates `hGateM` and `tGateM`, the three letters `ctGen` and the value of a word `ctEval`. SU(2) is Mathlib's `Matrix.specialUnitaryGroup`. Each theorem ends in `sorry`. The definitions are the ones in the proof files, with the same code.
- `SolovayKitaevLean/QuantumInfo/` and `SolovayKitaevLean/Analysis/` hold fifteen files copied from csd-lean4 at commit `30850da3`. Their imports are renamed from `CsdLean4.Mathlib` to `SolovayKitaevLean`. Each file says so at the top.
  - Their code is unchanged, with one exception: in `CliffordTAngle.lean`, `hGateM` and `tGateM` were moved earlier in the file. That makes their compiled definitions identical to `Challenge.lean`'s copies, which Comparator requires.
  - csd-lean4 uses Lean `v4.34.0` at that commit, and the code builds unchanged here.
  - Because the code is unchanged, the lemmas of `SolovayKitaevStep.lean` keep csd-lean4's namespace, `CSD.SolovayKitaev`.
  - I edited their comments to remove csd-lean4's internal notes and references to files that aren't here.
  - Three of them (`Register.lean`, `Pauli.lean`, `Clifford.lean`) aren't used by the theorems, and the theorems use one lemma from a fourth (`AmplitudeAmplification.lean`). They're here because `Magic.lean` imports them.
- `SolovayKitaevLean/Basic.lean` is new. It proves the two Challenge statements from the copied files.
- `Solution.lean` re-exports `SolovayKitaevLean`.

## Checks

- `audit/Axioms.lean` uses `#guard_msgs` to require that the two theorems and the key lemmas use only `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`.
- `audit/Control.lean`:
  - confirms that the audit detects `sorry`;
  - checks the gates entry by entry: H has entries ±1/√2 and determinant −1, and T = diag(1, e^(iπ/4)) has order exactly 8;
  - checks the letters: H is its own inverse, and the third letter T⁷ is the inverse of T;
  - checks that a word is evaluated in order: the word [T, H] has value T·H;
  - checks that the norm is the operator norm: a matrix's norm is the norm of the operator it defines on ℂ², and H has norm 1, which the entrywise and Frobenius norms would not give;
  - restates the theorem with the operator norm written out explicitly, and proves it from `solovay_kitaev`.
- `scripts/verify-comparator.sh` runs `lake comparator` in CI. It checks the Challenge against the proof and replays the proof through Lean's kernel, nanoda and con-ron. It needs Linux, so it doesn't run on my Windows machine. Lake's own comparison, `Lake.Check.compareAt`, does run here, and it accepts the proof for both theorems.
